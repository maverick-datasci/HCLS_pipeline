6 Pipeline Stages · Life Sciences Context
01
ICP Definition — The Pharma Buyer Map
Foundation
In life sciences, "who you're selling to" is more complex than any other vertical. A PBPK modelling platform sale at a top-10 pharma involves Computational Sciences, Drug Metabolism & Pharmacokinetics (DMPK), Regulatory CMC, and IT — each with different KPIs. AI analyses your best-fit accounts (e.g. AstraZeneca, GSK, Pfizer) and reverse-engineers the stakeholder map, buying triggers (new regulatory guidance, pipeline milestone, digital transformation programme), and deal-blocking personas.

02
Signal-Led Prospecting · Regulatory & Pipeline Triggers
Prospecting
In pharma, the best prospecting signals are not LinkedIn posts — they're FDA/EMA submissions, Phase II/III trial initiations, new regulatory guidances (e.g. EMA reflection papers on model-informed drug development), M&A activity, and digital transformation mandates. AI agents monitor these feeds daily and match events to your ICP, surfacing "why now" context for every outreach. A company filing an NDA has an immediate need for regulatory writing tools. A company entering Phase III needs PK/PD modelling support.

03
Scientifically-Grounded Personalised Outreach
Outreach
A generic "I help pharma companies with digital transformation" email gets deleted. A message that references a DMPK leader's recent publication on PBPK modelling, acknowledges a specific regulatory challenge they're navigating, and connects it to a capability — that earns a reply. AI generates this personalisation at scale: each message is grounded in scientific context, not marketing language. Crucially, all messages are reviewed before sending — the AI drafts, the scientist approves.
Claude (scientific writing)
Clay (auto-personalisation)
Instantly.ai (sending)
04
Multi-Stakeholder Qualification & Deal Architecture
Qualification
Pharma deals rarely have a single decision-maker. AI maps the buying committee — Champion (DMPK Scientist), Economic Buyer (VP R&D), Procurement, IT Security — and scores engagement across each. It tracks who has opened emails, attended webinars, and engaged with content. The system flags when a deal has a champion but no economic buyer contact (high-risk), or when IT has raised a data governance question (needs security documentation fast). Deal architecture, not just lead scoring.
HubSpot AI (deal scoring)
Salesforce Einstein
n8n (stakeholder routing)
05
Pre-Call Briefings & Scientific Discovery Intelligence
Conversion
Before every discovery call with a pharma prospect, an AI agent compiles a briefing: the account's pipeline assets and which are in PBPK-relevant development stages, recent publications by the stakeholder, competitive tools they currently use (Simcyp, GastroPlus, Phoenix), their regulatory submissions over the past 3 years, and likely objections. Post-call, AI transcribes, extracts regulatory-specific commitments and next steps, and surfaces if the prospect mentioned a competitor or a specific compliance concern — all logged to CRM with no manual entry.

06
Pipeline Health, Forecasting & Expansion Intelligence
Intelligence
Pharma enterprise deals move slowly but predictably. AI monitors deal velocity versus benchmark, flags stalled deals with specific recovery actions ("VP R&D hasn't been engaged for 3 weeks — send the new EMA AI guidance paper"), tracks land-expand signals within an account (a new therapeutic area launching = new modelling needs), and generates a weekly plain-English forecast — not a dashboard to interpret. It also identifies which accounts are approaching renewal and surfaces cross-sell opportunities based on their pipeline progression.
Clari (forecasting)

Safe Execution · Pharma & Regulated Industries
🔬
Scientific Accuracy Guardrails
Every AI-generated message referencing PBPK, PK/PD, regulatory guidance, or clinical data must be reviewed by someone with domain expertise before sending. Include explicit prompts: "Never state regulatory claims you cannot verify. Flag scientific statements for human review."
✋
Human-in-the-Loop — Always
In pharma sales, relationships are built on scientific credibility. Set all email tools to "draft mode" — AI prepares, you approve. Never let an agent send autonomously to VP/C-suite contacts. The first impression cannot be automated away.
🔒
GDPR & Data Governance
UK/EU GDPR applies strictly. Use work email addresses only, establish legitimate interest basis for B2B outreach, maintain suppression lists, and honour opt-outs immediately. Tools like Clay and Apollo are GDPR-compliant — verify before adding new data sources.
🏥
No Clinical or Compound Data in AI Tools
Commercial AI tools (Claude, HubSpot, Clay) must never receive proprietary compound data, unpublished clinical results, or patient-level information. Use AI only for publicly available information and internal commercial workflow data.
📋
Full Audit Trails
Every AI-generated touchpoint must be logged with timestamp and content in CRM. Pharma procurement often audits vendor communications — if asked "who sent what and when", you need complete, accurate records. HubSpot and Gong do this automatically.
🚦
Email Warm-Up & Domain Protection
Pharma spam filters are aggressive. New sending domains require 4–6 week warm-up before high-volume use (Instantly.ai automates this). Use separate domains from your primary corporate domain to protect deliverability to key accounts like GSK, AZ, Novartis.
🎯
Competitive Intelligence Ethics
AI can surface competitor product usage from public sources (job postings referencing "Simcyp", conference presentations, publications). Never use AI to access non-public competitive data. Position intelligence gathering as market understanding, not surveillance.
🛑
Kill Switches & Escalation Paths
If a regulatory contact replies negatively or a key account flags an issue, all sequences targeting that account must pause in under 60 seconds. Test this monthly. In pharma, one bad automated touchpoint to a decision-maker can cost a seven-figure opportunity.
Infrastructure philosophy: All of this runs on cloud SaaS — no servers to manage. For a life sciences commercial leader or small team, think of infrastructure as "which accounts connect to which, and who approves what before it moves."
